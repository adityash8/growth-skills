# Stalled-growth triage

Use when the agreed business metric is flat or falling. Compare like periods and check the measurement first. If a tracking change created the apparent decline, recompute on a consistent definition and reconsider whether the decline is real.

Pick the first applicable objective:

1. **Stop a real decline.** Name the cause and show that the proposed fix can close the gap. For customers, compare new customers with lost customers. For recurring revenue, compare new and expansion revenue against churn and contraction. Do not assume customer counts and revenue move together. A drop from 100 to 50 new customers per month will still shrink a 2,000-customer base if a churn fix leaves 60 losses per month.
2. **Fix activation before increasing acquisition.** If the core metric is not declining but most acquired users never reach first value, defer acquisition expansion and route the fix to `onboarding-activation`.
3. **Find the first supported constraint.** Check customer loss, price and positioning fit, expansion among retained customers, and channel saturation in that order. Mark a question unknown when evidence is missing; use the smallest diagnostic that could change the sequence. Check whether the business needs more growth before proposing a new market or channel.

For a customer base with a constant flow of new customers and a constant positive monthly churn rate, `new customers per month / monthly customer churn rate` is the implied ceiling. It is not a forecast. At zero churn there is no finite ceiling under that model if new customers keep arriving.

Report the selected objective, the evidence that put it first, the proposed move, and any other constraints or unknowns that would change the choice.

Framework influences: Jason Cohen on stalled growth, Elena Verna on fixing core decline, and Sean Ellis on LogMeIn activation, each interviewed on Lenny's Podcast.
