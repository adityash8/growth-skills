# Channel Fit Diagnostic

Choose the one primary acquisition channel, or at most two, that today's product can win; sequence the path from initial customers to a repeatable engine.

Use when choosing a channel, diagnosing stalled acquisition, assessing saturation, or considering a new platform. Do NOT use for quarterly planning (`growth-roadmap`), unit-economics grading (`saas-unit-economics-audit`), or product-led acquisition design (`product-led-acquisition`).

## Inputs

| Input | Needed detail |
|---|---|
| Product | Time to value; breadth of value; whether more users improve it; whether use creates unique content; transaction or subscription |
| Model | Revenue per customer and period, contract value, gross margin, retention, payback tolerance |
| Market | Total relevant customers, realistic capture share, revenue target and period |
| Channel mix | New customers by channel and period, spend, staff hours, acquisition cost, marginal cost and conversion quality |
| Demand | Search demand, category awareness, buyer discovery behavior |
| Motion | User, buyer, approval requirements, sales cycle, ability to adopt alone |
| Stage | Revenue, active users, paying customers, growth rate |
| History | Prior tests, audience, impressions, creative angles, spend, outcomes and dates |
| Capacity | Available people, creative/content supply, sales capacity, instrumentation and landing experience |
| Platform candidates | New platforms or surfaces under consideration |

Use supplied documents, exports and accessible public evidence. Continue with useful work when data is missing; label unknowns and explain which decisions they block. Never invent data, tool access, current platform availability or verified observations. Keep recommendations conditional when the evidence is insufficient.

## Evidence labels throughout the deliverable

Label every decision-driving quantity in every section:

- **Supplied/observed:** identify the input or source and its date/period.
- **Calculated:** show inputs, calculation, units and period.
- **Proposed/heuristic:** state the rationale; do not present it as a validated benchmark.

This includes budget moves, hours, test minimums, success/kill ranges, review dates and scale triggers, including numbers inherited from this framework. A clearly scoped legend may cover a table or the whole plan. Unknown quantities stay unknown. Audit all sections before finalizing.

## Procedure

### 1. Check concentration and saturation

Inspect the share of new customers by channel over comparable periods. As framework heuristics, growth concentrated in one channel can indicate fit, while no channel above half can signal scattered testing. Neither share proves fit without economics and customer-quality evidence.

Before scale, concentrate on the engine that works. At scale, prepare another engine before dependence becomes dangerous. Diversification should address breakage or saturation risk.

For each material channel, inspect volume at constant spend, reach/frequency, conversion per impression and marginal acquisition cost. Mark saturation **open**, **sagging**, **saturated**, or **unknown** when the evidence cannot establish a state.

A sagging or saturated channel outside the chosen bet needs a proposed cut in the verdict: current spend/hours, new spend/hours and the amount moved to the bet. A freeze or a quarter spent fixing it is not a cut. If allocations are missing, name the missing inputs rather than inventing amounts.

Paid dependence with flat organic acquisition is a risk, not proof of channel fit. Use any organic/paid mix benchmark as a dated heuristic, never a universal target.

### 2. Test model-market math and the economic ceiling

Calculate:

`revenue per customer × total relevant customers × realistic capture share`

Compare with the revenue target using consistent periods and units. Distinguish recurring revenue from lifetime revenue. Show the inputs and sensitivity to capture share.

If the math fails, stop channel recommendations: explain the model or market change needed first. Provide the evidence, missing inputs, risks and relevant handoff rather than forcing tests or a channel bet. If the inputs are unknown, show the formula and conditional scenarios; do not assert that the math passes.

Estimate sustainable acquisition cost from gross margin, retention and cash-payback tolerance, with assumptions and sensitivity ranges. Low revenue per customer usually requires low acquisition cost; sales or expensive paid media must be supported by the model. SEO also costs content, engineering and design time.

### 3. Choose the buying motion

Map who uses, pays and approves before ranking channels:

- User is buyer and can adopt without sign-off: product-led may fit.
- An engineer can independently adopt a platform product: developer-led may fit.
- Buyer differs from user, or security/procurement approval is needed: evaluate sales or hybrid adoption; self-serve signup alone may not close the purchase.

For bottom-up spread, test whether an individual gets value alone and cares about the craft, an audience community already exists, and the user connects to other roles. A proposed framework screen requires at least two of these three conditions; label that threshold heuristic. Plan the trigger for sales assistance as the segment changes.

### 4. Score channel prerequisites and operational fit

Score today's product with evidence for each pass/fail. Unknown evidence remains unknown. Put channels needing product changes in separate **buildable** rows.

| Channel | Prerequisites to test |
|---|---|
| Virality/referrals | Fast value, broad relevance, natural invitations or sharing, repeat use; network benefit strengthens fit |
| Paid social | Fast-to-understand value, creative supply, quick value after click, monetization that funds marginal cost |
| Paid search | Existing purchase intent, relevant landing experience, sustainable marginal acquisition cost |
| User-generated search content | Usage creates many unique useful pages; contributors have a reason to participate |
| Editorial/programmatic search | Real query demand, differentiated answers or tools, authority, capacity to maintain quality |
| Sales/outbound | Reachable specific buyers, contract value funding people, credible purchase process |
| Community/education | Repeated questions or interaction, peer density, trust and credible participation |
| Creator/partner distribution | Audience overlap, demonstrable value, incentives and repeatable access |
| AI discovery/new platforms | Audience presence, citable or useful output, verified distribution access and measurable qualified demand |

Score creative/content supply, sales capacity, instrumentation, landing experience and learning speed separately from product fit. Competitor activity is context, not proof that a channel will work.

Low usage frequency weakens habitual word of mouth. Subscription conversion and lifetime-value benchmarks are hypotheses to verify against this product. Existing conversion elsewhere can support a paid test but does not prove incrementality or scalability.

### 5. Identify natural distribution advantage

Map consumer versus business audience, existing search demand, and individual versus group use:

- High existing demand favors intent capture: search, useful listings and category discovery.
- Low existing demand needs education or demand creation: demonstrations, creators, community or awareness media.
- Natural invitations, public artifacts and network benefits can support product-driven sharing.
- Social incentives or referrals require a credible reason to share and sustainable economics.

Describe the demand × social-potential quadrant in the matrix. Features can unlock another channel later; score that separately from present fit.

### 6. Pre-register honest painted-door tests

Validate the selected channel cheaply before building the expensive system. Draft a clearly described waitlist page, demonstration, listing or bounded ad test; do not imply an unavailable feature exists.

Propose two or three tests **within the selected channel**, with cost, staff time, owner, timebox, minimum impressions or qualified contacts, creative angles, success range, kill criterion and re-evaluation date. Label proposed thresholds and explain their rationale. For creative tests, two or three angles are a framework heuristic, not a measured requirement.

Do not reject a channel until its test clears the registered minimum and misses the success range. If minimums are not reached, record insufficient evidence. One failed test is not a permanent verdict. A waitlist needs a named traffic source; it is not a channel by itself.

All tests remain drafts until the approval rule below is met.

### 7. Evaluate a new platform only when relevant

Date the assessment and verify current availability, audience eligibility, policies and measurement limitations from current sources. Check changing AI discovery and ad surfaces rather than assuming historical availability. Without web access, identify unverified claims and make the recommendation conditional.

Place the platform in its cycle: category conditions emerging → defensible advantage found → access opens → access closes or becomes taxed.

Assess retention/engagement, monetizable audience quality, builder value exchange and scale. Check overlap between customer need, company goal and channel strengths; platform stage and monetization; company risk tolerance, spare staff and core-channel volume.

Plan the exit at entry: own a workflow step, specialized data/context or a network among your users. No exit plan or defined keep-going signal means no bet.

An early company makes one focused bet. A mature company may support more if capacity permits. The framework proposes a maximum of one quarter and an initial directional review around a month; label these as heuristics and distinguish directional signal from statistical proof.

### 8. Sequence and scale the engine

Name the current stage explicitly:

**Kickstart → growth engine → lubricants → turbo boost → mid-stage accelerants.**

Zero customers/users is the kickstart stage. Kickstarts acquire initial customers through founder effort; an engine repeatedly produces more acquisition input from its output. Lubricants improve conversion, onboarding or pricing once an engine runs. Turbo boosts are temporary spikes. Partnerships and geographic expansion can accelerate an established engine.

Name the fuel: money for paid/sales (measure payback), content for search, users for viral acquisition (measure invitations and conversion). Start preparing engine two before engine one flattens or loses access. Hire a channel specialist after there is evidence of a repeatable engine.

Scale against pre-registered marginal-cost, quality and payback limits. The source framework suggests testing larger increases rather than tiny increments; any proposed increase, return target or optimization horizon must be labelled heuristic and must fit this product's economics. Do not scale from average cost while marginal cost rises.

At kickstart, add a plan card:

- Narrow audience: three proposed defining traits, one dense gathering place, estimated size and one signup trait to track.
- Existing distribution advantages: audience, relationships, story, sharing loop, platform access or founder effort.
- Constraint: consumer density, business-buyer trust, or marketplace supply/demand balance.
- Choose two or three tactics, name influential audience hubs, offer a concrete customer gain first, and assign an owner, timebox and success number.
- Consumer options: audience gathering places, targeted outreach, relevant friends, creators, stories/content or physical placement. Business options: relevant network, targeted outbound, investor introductions, communities, content, then press/launch; prioritize trust.
- Friends qualify only when they match the audience. Log friend-sourced users separately. Partnerships generally follow proof.
- At kickstart, use paid only for a bounded painted-door demand test, not ongoing scaled acquisition.
- Propose a review of usage/retention by audience traits at the first meaningful cohort; the source heuristic is around 100 users or 10 paying customers. Label it proposed. Change the audience focus if another group retains better.

If the audience or gathering place cannot be identified, hand off to `customer-research` before prescribing kickstart tactics.

## Output contract

Lead with the verdict in the recipient's metric, such as acquisition cost or payback. Date the diagnostic. Apply the evidence labels to every section.

1. **Verdict:** one primary channel, at most two; current named stage; confidence; proposed budget/time move. Every non-bet channel consuming money or staff gets stop or cut, with amounts moved. Hold/cap/maintain is not a decision. Unknown allocations stay unknown and conditional.
2. **Fit matrix:** candidate channel, prerequisites with pass/fail/unknown, model/economic fit, buying motion, demand/social quadrant, current share of growth, saturation, operational fit, and bet/painted-door/drop decision.
3. **Economic ceiling:** model-market calculation, sustainable acquisition-cost range, periods, assumptions and sensitivities.
4. **Validation plan:** bounded tests with registered minimums, success/kill rules, owner and review/re-evaluation dates.
5. **Sequence:** current stage → next component → trigger for preparing a second engine; add the kickstart card when relevant.
6. **Platform bet card, if relevant:** dated cycle step and evidence, entry assessment, customer/channel/company fit, exit plan, people/budget cap, first review, keep-going and kill ranges.
7. **Drop list and risks:** channels stopped/cut, platform/auction/algorithm/partner dependencies, missing evidence and proportionate mitigation.

When model-market math fails, the stop finding replaces the channel bet and test plan; do not fabricate a full positive diagnosis.

## Handoffs

Use only available skills. Describe the needed specialist work in plain language when no matching skill exists; do not invent a sibling or claim it ran.

- Economics and spend review → `saas-unit-economics-audit`.
- Natural invitations, sharing surfaces and adoption → `product-led-acquisition`; viral-loop design and math → `viral-loop`.
- Quarterly sequencing → `growth-roadmap`.
- Missing audience/buyer evidence → `customer-research`.
- Test design and decision rules → `experiment-design`.
- Paid creative and campaign operations → `paid-media-creative-operations`; paid performance review → `paid-analysis-review`.
- Conversational advertising execution, when available → `conversational-ads-optimization`.

## Guardrails

- All budget changes and tests are proposals only. Obtain explicit human approval of the **specific budget, scope and timing** before changing a live budget, spending money, publishing a page or listing, or launching a live test. Economics checks and pre-registration do not authorize execution. Without approval, deliver drafts and recommendations only.
- Concentrate learning within the chosen channel; do not recommend testing many channels at once.
- Do not infer fit from low-volume results or treat attribution software as proof of incrementality.
- Do not use a channel the model cannot fund, or score prerequisites from aspirations.
- Re-run the diagnosis when platform rules, pricing, purchase behavior or the product changes.
