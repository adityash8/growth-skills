# Channel Fit Diagnostic

Use this skill when choosing a primary acquisition channel, diagnosing a channel that will not scale, or deciding whether to diversify.

## Inputs

- product value, time to value, sharing surface, and purchase path;
- audience size, concentration, demand awareness, and buying behavior;
- price, gross margin, retention, sales complexity, and target economics;
- current channel mix, marginal cost, conversion quality, and saturation;
- realistic revenue outcome and available capacity.

## Procedure

1. **Check model-market math.** Test whether realistic customers × reachable share × revenue per customer can support the outcome. If not, no channel fixes the model.
2. **Define the economic ceiling.** Estimate the maximum sustainable acquisition cost from gross margin, retention, and cash-payback tolerance. Use sensitivity ranges.
3. **Score product prerequisites.** For each channel, test whether the product has the attributes the channel rewards:
   - existing intent for search and marketplaces;
   - broad, visual, fast-to-understand value for paid social;
   - high contract value and narrow reachable buyers for sales or outbound;
   - recurring questions and differentiated expertise for content;
   - natural invitations, collaboration, or public artifacts for product-led distribution;
   - credible peer density and repeated interaction for community.
4. **Score operational fit.** Check creative supply, sales capacity, content authority, instrumentation, landing experience, and speed of learning.
5. **Identify the power-channel candidate.** Prefer the channel with structural fit and an observable early signal. Do not reward a channel merely because competitors use it.
6. **Run a painted-door test.** Build the smallest honest test of demand or distribution before the expensive system. Define cost, timebox, success threshold, and invalidation condition.
7. **Sequence the engine.** Distinguish initial kickstart work, the repeatable engine, conversion improvements, one-off boosts, and the trigger for testing a second engine.
8. **Map platform risk.** State which external rule, auction, algorithm, or partner could break the engine and what hedge is proportionate.

## Output contract

1. **Verdict:** primary channel, current stage, and confidence.
2. **Fit matrix:** channel × product, market, model, operations, economics.
3. **Economic ceiling:** assumptions and sensitivity range.
4. **Validation plan:** two or three bounded tests with decision rules.
5. **Sequence:** kickstart → engine → optimization → second-engine trigger.
6. **Drop list:** channels that should not receive more time now.
7. **Platform risk:** dependency and mitigation.

## Handoffs

- paid-channel economics → `saas-unit-economics-audit`;
- natural invitations or shareable outputs → `product-led-acquisition`;
- sequenced quarter plan → `growth-roadmap`.

## Guardrails

- Do not recommend testing many channels at once.
- Do not infer fit from a single low-volume result.
- Do not scale from average cost when marginal cost is rising.
- Do not treat attribution software as proof of incrementality.
- Do not use a high-cost channel when the model cannot fund it.
- Re-run the diagnosis when platform rules, pricing, or product behavior changes.
