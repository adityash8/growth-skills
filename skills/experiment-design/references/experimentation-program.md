# Experimentation program: audit and operating guide

Use for a program-shaped request. Keep single-test execution in [the main skill](../SKILL.md). No standing meeting schedule, launch quota or portfolio target is assumed.

## 1. Establish the decision system

Record the goal, decision rights already supplied, eligible populations, measurement quality, available traffic, outcome delays and actual implementation capacity. Mark missing owners or dates unknown. Check assignment, exposure and metric definitions before optimizing velocity.

Build an evidence-based red/amber/green scorecard for hypothesis quality, measurement validity, design/power, safety/ethics, decision follow-through and reusable learning. For each rating show the observed evidence and the smallest repair; unknown is not green.

## 2. Collect hypotheses and maintain a searchable backlog

Use funnel drop-offs, customer interviews, support questions, heatmaps/recordings, competitor patterns and past positive/negative/inconclusive experiments as inputs. Competitor behavior is a question, not proof. Capture:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Observation and source | Measured issue or explicitly unverified hypothesis |
| Hypothesis | Because observation, change should affect outcome for audience |
| Decision | What changes after positive, negative or inconclusive evidence |
| Evidence / prediction | Source quality and predicted effect with units |
| Design feasibility | Unit, baseline, MDE, alpha/power, traffic and outcome delay |
| Risk / effort | Safety, guardrails, running costs and dependencies |
| Links / status | Prior tests, artifact, keywords and decision record |

Hand ranking and re-ranking to **growth-roadmap** with this evidence. Do not add a competing scoring formula. Revisit priority when evidence, constraints or strategy changes, rather than on an invented calendar.

## 3. Execute and decide

Run Step 0 for each candidate. Precommit sample, stopping rules, multiplicity family, green/red actions and inconclusive handling. Check validity before efficacy; keep a searchable log of invalid tests and their causes. Track idea-to-decision time, time blocked and sample feasibility to identify bottlenecks without rewarding premature calls.

For delayed effects, propose a randomized holdout only when feasible and decision-relevant. Specify the unit, interference assumptions, exposure duration, outcome window, attrition checks and cost of withholding treatment. Repeated holdout reads need a valid analysis plan. Observational retention trends do not replace a randomized comparison.

## 4. Promote supported patterns to a playbook

Use `test-templates.md`. Each entry includes hypothesis, variants, dates actually supplied, assignment/analysis unit, sample and exclusions, absolute and relative effects with intervals, guardrails, net value, decision, remaining uncertainty, segment caveats and links. Separate a confirmed pattern from an explanation that is still a hypothesis. Record where it might transfer and what must be retested; a winner is specific to its context.

## 5. Learn from prediction errors

Compare predictions with results once valid readouts are available. Investigate surprising wins and losses, denominator changes, implementation failures and underpowered studies. Inconclusive does not mean equivalent or permanently disproven. Keep all outcomes searchable by surface, audience and mechanism so the next idea can reuse the evidence.

## Program-audit output

Return the evidence scorecard, ranked repair proposals, holdout feasibility and a capacity-based portfolio proposal. State unknowns, explicit stop conditions and decision rights. Monitor validity-failure rate, mature outcome coverage, implementation follow-through and guardrail harm alongside throughput. Do not compound relative lifts across overlapping populations as if every gain were independent and durable.
